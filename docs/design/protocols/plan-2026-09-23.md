## 1. What this is

**Protocols** are four approval processes that move through stages: tournament, product release,
hiring, and price or promotion change. The owner can shape each one lightly. Around them sits a
**staff area in the mobile app**. Every staff account signs in there and does its hands-on work:
- daily checklists
- new-item proposals
- production
- the shopping list and driver purchases
- marketing's own take on products, launches, tournaments and campaigns (not customer feedback),
  and campaign drafts
- staff requests
- the protocol steps that belong to them

The operator desktop app stays the place for managing: step templates, decisions, purchase review.
It is also the only place for the till, the desk and the kitchen board. Staff and the owner get
phone push when something is theirs to do.

**Scope and contract.** None of this is in the signed Phase 1 Scope of Work, and none of it is in
the Phase 2 change order, which is still unsigned. The SOW change-control clause
(`docs/scope/touch-padel-phase1-scope-of-work.txt:956-963`) requires a written line with its effect
on date and fee before work starts. This work touches four exclusions:
- **Roles beyond the five.** ui-spec:527 says "additional roles are a change request". The six
  roles in `f6a0802` are already on `main`, so they need recording now.
- **Purchase orders and supplier ordering.** SOW:548-556 says "goods received only", and ui-spec:687
  says the same. Shopping list and driver purchases stop short of ordering: they record what was
  bought and hand it to Goods in.
- **Staff shift scheduling or time clock.** SOW:265. Checklists stay task lists, and no report
  ranks people (ui-spec:687, SOW:480).
- **Tournaments.** This is change-order item 4, Milestone 6. The tournament protocol is its
  planning front half only.

The change order lists nine numbered items. It also names a dropped tenth item, AI analysis, which
was never given a number and "comes back as its own change order". The day-30 AI write-up (#22) is
not that item, but it sits close to it, so the line must name it. It reuses the edge-function
pattern of `analytics-insights` (gate, template fallback, metering), not the owner assistant, which
stays off. Proposed line for `docs/scope/phase2-change-order-2026-09-21.md` §2, numbered 10 as the
next row of that table:

## 3. Where everything lives

Rule of thumb (**PROPOSAL**, not decided): **each step form exists in exactly one app.**
- The phone gets steps whose actor is a bar, kitchen, driver or marketing role, plus every
  owner-added generic step.
- The operator gets manager and owner step forms, plus all decisions and all editing. The reason is
  that these forms are long and full of numbers. Default #32 puts "doing the work" on the phone,
  and a manager filling in plan, price or launch is doing protocol work, so §11 Q8 asks whether
  those forms should also be on the phone.
- The desk gets the court-blocking step.
- **Starting a run is not a step form.** A run's start form may appear in both apps:
  - price or promotion change: marketing on the phone (`staff-change.tsx`), manager and owner on
    either app;
  - product release: the phone only (`staff-proposal.tsx`);
  - tournament and hiring: the operator only.

  Both apps call the same `start_protocol`.

### 4.1 Data model (0162)

```

                        variant text check (variant is null or variant in ('type1','type2','type3')),
                        name_en text not null, name_ar text not null,
                        version int not null default 1, updated_by uuid → staff, updated_at timestamptz,

                        submitted_by uuid not null → staff, submitted_at timestamptz not null,
```

- **Snapshot.** `start_protocol` copies the template's steps and items into `protocol_run_steps`
  and `protocol_run_items` and stores `template_version`. Every later edit to the template affects
  only new runs. Customising one run (for example, the lists for one tournament) edits only that
  run's rows (§4.5).
- **Title.** The run title shows on the desk calendar (§5.2) and in both languages' UIs.
  - When the **owner** starts a run, `start_protocol` requires both `title_en` and `title_ar`
    (`TEXT_BOTH_LANGUAGES_REQUIRED`, decision #19).
  - Anyone else may type one language, and the UI shows it in both. This is a **PROPOSAL**: #19
    covers owner-typed text only. §11 Q10 asks whether staff-typed titles need both.
  - A tournament's desk label uses its public name, which is EN and AR required anyway (§5.2).
- **Deleting a draft item.** `menu_item_id` is `on delete set null`, so a stopped product release
  can delete its draft item (§5.1). The other children of `menu_items` already cascade
  (0013:38, 65, 79, 85-86; 0027:82).
- **Venue.** Every table carries `venue_id`, directly or through its FK.
  - Templates are seeded by 0162 for every active venue: four kinds, with three tournament variants.
  - A new venue copies venue 1's templates (the `PHASE-2-PLAN.md:25` rule).
  - Root RPCs take an optional `p_venue_id` and fall back to `app.current_venue()`. How it is
    checked depends on the guard:
    - **Any-staff RPCs** (`my_protocol_work`, `protocol_step_detail`, `my_checklists_today`, the
      release-note RPCs, the `staff-media` INSERT policy) guard with `app.staff_role() is null`,
      then check `p_venue_id = any(app.staff_venue_ids())`. `app.is_staff_at` takes an explicit
      role list (0139:269), and `packages/db/CLAUDE.md` forbids a list of every role.
    - **Subset RPCs** (manager and owner, kitchen, and so on) use `app.is_staff_at(p_venue_id,
      <roles>)`.
  - The scheduler passes `venue_id` explicitly and never relies on `current_venue()`, which raises
    `VENUE_REQUIRED` once a second venue is live (0125).
- **Reads.** RLS select only for manager and owner at the venue (`venue_id = any(app.staff_venue_ids())`,
  owner global). Everyone else reads through definer RPCs that return only what involves them. No
  direct write grants.
- **Typed records.** `record` and `data` are jsonb, but each `step_key` has a validator
  (`app.protocol_record_valid(step_key, record)`, raising `RECORD_INVALID` with the field name). The
  0072 lesson, typed payloads rather than free jsonb, is kept in code instead of CHECK constraints,
  because the step list is editable.

### 4.2 Built-in ("system") steps vs owner-added generic steps

- **A system step** has a `step_key`. It has its own record fields and a behaviour that runs when it
  **passes** (approved or auto):
  - create the draft menu item
  - record the test stock
  - write the price
  - launch
  - block courts
  - open Add staff
  - apply a price or promotion

  The owner may rename it (EN and AR), flip "Needs my OK", edit its checklist, and mark it optional
  where §5 allows. **PROPOSAL** (Majed chose full step editing, §2 #7): the owner cannot delete it
  or change its actor roles, because the behaviour's guard depends on them. §11 Q7 asks whether
  that is acceptable, or whether the owner should be able to change a built-in step's actors, for
  example handing the marketing step to the manager.
- **A generic step** (`step_key` null) is added by the owner. It has:
  - a name in EN and AR
  - actor roles picked from the 9 live non-owner roles (prep is retired, 0157)
  - "Needs my OK"
  - an optional flag
  - checklist items

  Its record is a note, optional photos and the ticks. It has no behaviour. It is done on the phone.
  Manager and owner can also submit it on the operator (note and ticks, no photo).

### 4.3 State machine (the 0112 transition-table pattern)

Two pure `immutable` functions, `app.protocol_step_allowed(from, to)` and
`app.protocol_run_allowed(from, to)`, have the same shape as `app.assistant_job_allowed`
(0112:30-41). Every write goes through one RPC that checks them and raises `INVALID_TRANSITION`,
which `op/lib/errors.ts` already maps.

```
step:  waiting ──(becomes current)──▶ open ──(submit)──▶ submitted ──(approve)─────────────────▶ passed
          │                            ▲                     │ ├──(author = decider: auto)──────▶ passed  (decision 'auto')
          │                            │                     │ ├──(send back + reason)──────────▶ open, round+1
          │                            └──(withdraw)─────────┘ │     or an earlier step reopens (steps between → waiting)
          │                                                    └──(stop + reason)───────────────▶ stopped ⇒ run stopped
          └──(skip + reason; optional steps only; decider)──▶ skipped
       passed ──(send back from a LATER step)──▶ open, round+1        -- the only way out of passed

run:   active ──(last blocking step passes, "now")───────▶ done | live (product release)
       active ──(last blocking step passes, "on date")───▶ scheduled ──(date, ready)──▶ done | live
       scheduled ──(date, not ready | manager cancels the date)──▶ active (last step reopens, manager pushed)
       live ──(day-30 review written)──▶ done
       active ──(a step stopped)──▶ stopped
       active ──(starter withdraws before the first decision)──▶ withdrawn
```

- Each submit adds a new `protocol_submissions` row. A sent submission is never edited. The
  submitter withdraws it instead, while it is undecided (0072:12-29).
- A send-back to an earlier step keeps every earlier round in history. The test loop in product
  release is this mechanism.
- No due dates or reminders in v1.

### 4.4 Who decides (the 0072 rules, extended)

| Situation | Decider |
|---|---|
| `needs_owner_ok` on | The owner. |
| `needs_owner_ok` off | Any active manager at the run's venue. |
| Submitter is the decider: a manager submits a manager-decided step (decision #9), or the owner submits an owner-decided step (**PROPOSAL**; brief G6, never answered) | Nobody. The step passes automatically, with `decision = 'auto'`, `decided_by = submitted_by`, and an audit row `protocol.step.auto`. |

- **Nobody decides their own submission.** The table CHECK and the RPC (`CANNOT_DECIDE_OWN`, reused
  from 0072:237) enforce it. The automatic pass is the one recorded exception.
- **A reason is required** on send-back, stop and skip (`REASON_REQUIRED`, reused from 0072:243).
  Send-back and stop keep it in `protocol_submissions.decision_note`. A skip usually has no
  submission, so it goes in `protocol_run_steps.skip_note`, with `skipped_by` and `skipped_at`.
- **One-way.** No un-approve. A later step can send the work back, and that is recorded as a new
  round.
- **The owner may override** (**PROPOSAL**; brief P4, never answered). At the price step the owner
  may change the number. `owner_override` keeps the owner's figures, and the submission keeps the
  manager's. The alternative is approve or send back only.
- **Who may act.** An actor is anyone holding one of `actor_roles` at the venue, or the
  `assigned_to` person when it is set; the test step is assigned to the proposer. Manager and owner
  may always cover, and the record shows who submitted.

### 4.5 What the owner can edit, and the limits

"How it works" is the owner-only sheet on each Protocols card (§7.1). It edits the template:
- step names (EN and AR)
- "Needs my OK"
- "Optional", only where the step's definition allows it
- checklist items: add, rename, remove, reorder, up to 12 per step, EN and AR
- generic steps: add, remove, pick actor roles
- order, using `SortButtons` (`op/components/inputs.tsx`)

`app.save_protocol_template(p_template_id, p_expected_version, p_steps jsonb)` (owner only) checks
the whole list, then bumps `version`. The first two limits below are a **PROPOSAL** that narrows
decision #7 (§11 Q7). Only two pairs of built-in steps can swap: price and marketing in product
release, and marketing and courts in a tournament. Every other built-in step keeps its place.
- **First and last are fixed.** The first system step (propose, plan, open position) stays first.
  The terminal system step (launch, ready, add staff, apply) stays last. Product release's
  `post_launch` is a fixed tail that is not in the reorderable list.
- **System steps keep their dependencies.** Each declares `after` keys (§5). A move that breaks one
  is disabled in the editor and refused by the server with `PROTOCOL_ORDER_INVALID`. Generic steps
  move freely between the first and the terminal step.
- **Every text is bilingual.** `TEXT_BOTH_LANGUAGES_REQUIRED`.
- **Concurrent edits fail.** `TEMPLATE_CHANGED` when `p_expected_version` is stale.

**Per-run customisation** is a **PROPOSAL**. Nobody decided it: the brief's G2 sub-question, "who
may change the checklist on one running protocol", was never answered, and §11 Q11 asks it. The
proposal is that the manager and owner may edit one run:
- edit its checklist items (`edit_run_items`, EN and AR required);
- add a generic step (`add_run_step`) before its terminal step.

They cannot remove or reorder system steps on a run. An edit to a step that already passed does
not reopen it.

  then shows "cost unknown" instead of a margin.

  2. Every active size has a price greater than 0.

  4. Every size has a recipe.

    1. copies the chosen photo from `staff-media` to `menu-media/items/<item_id>/<run_id>.<ext>`,

       run to `live`.

    re-checks readiness when the time arrives, then does the same thing.
  - The cross-bucket copy uses `storage.copy` with `destinationBucket`. **UNVERIFIED** on the
    hosted storage version. The fallback is a download plus an upload inside the same function.
- **Nobody can go around the gate.** 0164 re-issues `app.upsert_menu_item` (latest body 0054) so
  that `is_active = true` on an item whose release run is not `live` or `done` raises

  cascade, and the run's own `menu_item_id` is `on delete set null` (§4.1), so the delete cannot fail
  on the run. Its photos are removed 90 days after the stop.

  - **Staff notes (PROPOSAL, §11 Q9).** For 30 days any staff member at the venue can add a note on
    the item from the phone (`release_notes`: author, text, time). Nobody decided this: the

    observations, not a log of what guests said (#14). If Q9 drops them, the write-up reads
    marketing's own take (`marketing_notes` whose subject is the item or the run) instead.
  - On day 30 the scheduler builds the numbers review from existing reads and stores it in
    `release_reviews.numbers`: units, revenue, margin, share of category, days sold, bought-with
    (the `report_cafe` item rows, 0096; `v_item_margin`).
  - The `release-review` edge function then writes the AI write-up. **The model** follows the
    assistant's switch, not `analytics-insights`:
    - `_shared/assistant/provider.ts` `vendorFor` on the venue's `llm_default_model` (0140; 0142
      set it to a Groq model for now).
    - The setting is read with the venue qualified, never from unqualified `venue_settings`
      (`packages/db/CLAUDE.md`).
    - `analytics-insights` itself calls Groq directly with `GROQ_API_KEY` (`index.ts:68-70`) and
      never reads that setting. `packages/db/CLAUDE.md` makes `claude-opus-5` the default for new
      LLM code unless Parsa names another.
    - §11 Q13 records which model decision covers release-review.

    Taken from `analytics-insights` (`functions/analytics-insights/index.ts:1-33`) are only:
    - the template fallback when no key is set;
    - the number gate, so any figure not in the payload is dropped;
    - metering through `app.llm_record_usage` under the spend cap (0079, 0149).

    Any notes go to the model as text only, with no names.

    enough data yet. Majed should expect that for slow items.
  - **Measurable campaigns only.** A campaign-effect claim is made only when the launch campaign
    carries a promotion (`op/features/marketing/MarketingPanel.tsx:9-17`).

- Court analytics subtract maintenance from open capacity (0097:280-290), so a tournament reads as
  a closed court.
- Guests see "blocked" (`packages/core/src/availability/slotGrid.ts:111`).
- A four-court, two-day event takes eight separate, non-atomic submissions.

- **Keep `kind = 'maintenance'`.** Every conflict guard, the opening-hours exemption and the guest
  rendering stay unchanged. A new `reservation_kind` value would touch about 16 migrations' checks
  and 17 client files, and a desktop build that does not know it would render it as a booking.
- **Add** `reservations.block_purpose text` (nullable, no default) with
  `check (block_purpose is null or block_purpose = 'event')`, and
  `reservations.protocol_run_id uuid → protocol_runs`. The CHECK and the FK are added NOT VALID then
  VALIDATE inside a `pg_constraint` guard scoped by `conrelid`.
  - **No backfill.** An ordinary desk block made after 0166 still goes through
    `staff_create_reservation` (`CourtBlock.tsx:128-134` → `op/lib/mutate.ts` `'reservation.create'`;
    latest body 0147), which does not know the column, so it would get NULL anyway.
  - A backfill UPDATE would also fire the `reservations_rt` trigger (0022:94-96) once per
    historical block.
  - Only `block_courts_for_event` writes `'event'`. "Maintenance" means
    `kind = 'maintenance' and block_purpose is distinct from 'event'`.
- **`app.block_courts_for_event(p_run_id, p_blocks jsonb, p_idempotency_key)`** (court_desk,
  manager, owner):
  - takes every `app.lock_court` in court-id order;
  - writes all the blocks in one transaction, or refuses with the conflicting slots listed;
  - the desk then clears those through the normal move and cancel path, and a guest cancellation
    still sends `booking_cancelled`.
- **Court analytics.** `app.analytics_open_minutes` (latest 0097:236-305) subtracts only
  `kind = 'maintenance' and block_purpose is distinct from 'event'`, so event minutes stay in open
  capacity. An "events" line of its own costs more than a re-issue:
  - the function's return type is fixed (`court_id, dow, hour, open_minutes, open_days`) and it is
    granted to `service_role` only;

    0147:543, call at :588) and `analytics_courts_cafe` (latest 0147:1098, call at :1132);
  - the operator analytics types change with them.

  If that is too much for v1, cut the events line and only stop subtracting event minutes. Whether
  event minutes should count as occupied instead is §11 Q5.
- **Label.** The desk calendar label shows the tournament's public name, which exists in EN and AR

### 6.1 Architecture

**Sign-in.** Staff use the email segment of `/sign-in` (`app/sign-in.tsx:131-172`). `staff-admin`
already creates staff with `email_confirm: true`. Today a staff sign-in falls into
`/complete-profile`, because the staff member's `phone` is NULL (`RequireNoSession.tsx:53-54`), and
then `/accept-terms`. The fix:
- `postSignInStep` (`src/features/auth/social.ts:198-204`) reads the caller's own `staff` row
  alongside the profile. The RLS own-row rule (0004:173-174) allows it.
- It returns a new `'staff'` step, writes the device hint and replaces to `/staff`.

**`StaffStatusProvider`** (`src/features/staff/`) is mounted inside `AuthProvider` at
`app/_layout.tsx:375-381`.
- **States:** `none | pending | guest | staff{role, displayName, venues} | revoked | unsupported`.
  The default is `guest`, so every existing suite renders unchanged.
- **Classification.** It uses `STAFF_ROLES` and `resolveStaffRow` / `nextStaff`, moved from
  `op/lib/roleResolution.ts` into `@touch/core`. Both apps already depend on it, so the role list
  cannot drift.
  - Today `resolveStaffRow` returns `revoked` both for a missing row and for an unknown role
    (`roleResolution.ts:105, 107`). The moved function gets a distinct result for an unknown role
    (`unknown_role`). The operator keeps mapping that to revoked, so `roleResolution.test.ts` holds.
  - On the phone, every guest session reads its own staff row and gets none, so "missing row" can
    only mean revoked when the phone knows this uid was staff. The mobile mapping:

    | What the read returns | Status |
    |---|---|
    | An error | Unknown: keep the previous answer |
    | No row, and no staff hint for this uid | `guest` |
    | No row, with a staff hint for this uid | `revoked` |
    | A row with `is_active = false` | `revoked` |
    | An active row with a role this build does not know (`unknown_role`) | `unsupported`, which shows "Update the app". Unlike the operator, it is not read as revoked. |
    | An active row with a known role | `staff` |
- **Re-check.** On focus, plus every 60 s while the status is staff (`ROLE_RECHECK_MS`).
- **Device hint** `{uid, surface: 'staff'}`:
  - stored in AsyncStorage and read in the pre-splash `bootPrefs`, so a staff cold start never
    mounts the guest tabs;
  - cleared on `SIGNED_OUT` (`src/features/auth/context.tsx:68-77`) and on revoked;
  - every access wrapped in try/catch.

**Routes.** Flat files on the root stack with search params, following the `/review` precedent. No
new layout, so `routes.test.ts:48-53` holds.

### 6.7 Sign-in hardening

Decision #33 (a default, not objected) covers three things only: email and password only, social
sign-in refused, and owner-only password resets. "Revoked" and "One account per phone" below are
not part of it. "Revoked" is a **PROPOSAL**, and "One account per phone" is a consequence of #27.

- **Email and password only** (#33). If a Google or Apple sign-in resolves to an active staff row, the
  app signs out and says "Staff accounts sign in with email and password". GoTrue links providers
  by verified email (`social.ts:222-229`), so this refusal is client-side. **UNVERIFIED:** whether a
  server-side check on the session's `amr` in staff RPCs is practical; it is a follow-up, not v1.
- **Password resets are owner-only** (#33). The staff home does not link to change-password or
  forgot-password; the owner resets on the Staff page.
  - The mobile minimum is 8 characters against staff-admin's 10 (`staff-admin/index.ts:33`), and
    the staff area never reaches those screens.
  - GoTrue will still send a reset email to anyone who asks for any address. Blocking that needs
    the Send Email auth hook. **UNVERIFIED**, noted for the security pass.
- **Revoked** (**PROPOSAL**). A full-screen "This staff account is switched off" with Sign out, never a fall back
  to the guest UI. An access token lives up to 3600 s after 0081; the 60 s re-check covers that
  tail.
- **One account per phone** (a consequence of #27). Signing in as staff moves the push token to the staff profile, so the
  person's guest booking reminders pause (`src/features/auth/api.ts:176-183`). The Account section
  says so.

## 11. Open questions

1. **Billing and order.** Is line 10 a billed change-order item or a recorded vendor addition? Does
   it run before Milestone 1 slice 2 or alongside it (they compete for ordinals)?
2. **Deciding from the phone.** Push tells the owner and manager something is waiting, but the
   default puts every decision on the operator. Should approve, send back and stop also work on the
   phone? Tasting a test at the bar is the obvious case.
3. **Type 2 tournaments.** With feasibility skipped, is there any owner approval? The default is
   no: the manager decides, and a manager's own plan passes automatically.
4. **Direct price edits.** Once the price/promo protocol exists, may managers still change prices
   directly in the menu editor, as they can today?
5. **Event hours in court analytics.** Should the hours blocked for an event count as occupied, or
   only show on their own line (the default)?
6. **Review login.** Is it acceptable for the App Review staff login to see the venue's real
   shopping list, or should there be a demo venue?
7. **How far "full step editing" goes.** Under the proposal, built-in steps cannot be deleted and
   cannot change who does them. Their order is fixed apart from two swaps: price and marketing in
   product release, and marketing and courts in a tournament. Is that acceptable? Or should the
   owner also be able to change a built-in step's actors, for example handing the marketing step to
   the manager?
8. **The manager's own step forms on the phone.** Default #32 puts doing the work on the phone. Should
   the manager's step forms (plan, feasibility, price, numbers, open position, interviews, launch,
   apply) also be fillable on the phone, or stay on the operator (the proposal, because they are
   long and numeric)?
9. **Staff notes on new items.** Should every staff member be able to add notes on a new item for
   30 days, feeding the day-30 write-up? Or should marketing's own take be the only input? Either
   way these are staff's own observations, never a log of guest feedback.
10. **Run titles.** The owner's titles need EN and AR (#19). Do titles typed by staff (a head
    barista's proposal, marketing's price change) need both too?
11. **Changing one running protocol.** May the manager edit one run's checklists and add steps to
    it, or only the owner (or nobody)?
12. **Launch and the owner's powers.** Who presses Launch, the manager (the proposal) or the owner?
    May the owner change the manager's price at the price and numbers steps? When the owner
    submits a step only the owner decides, should it pass automatically, as a manager's own step
    does under #9?
13. **Model for the day-30 write-up.** Does release-review follow the venue's `llm_default_model`
    (a Groq model since 0142), or `packages/db/CLAUDE.md`'s default for new LLM code,
    `claude-opus-5` unless Parsa names another?
