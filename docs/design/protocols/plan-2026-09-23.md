## 1. What this is

**Protocols** are four approval processes that move through stages: tournament, product release,
hiring, and price or promotion change. The owner can shape each one lightly. Around them sits a
**staff area in the mobile app**. Every staff account signs in there and does its hands-on work:
- daily checklists
- new-item proposals
- production
- the shopping list and driver purchases
- marketing's own take on products, launches, tournaments and campaigns (not customer feedback),
  and campaign drafts
- staff requests
- notes on newly launched items, including what customers said
- the protocol steps that belong to them, and, for the manager and owner, the decisions

Every protocol step form, every run start and every decision works in **both** apps, the phone and
the operator desktop (#38). The operator stays the place for managing: editing templates and
checklists, changing one running protocol, and receiving the driver's purchases into stock. It is
also the only place for the till, the desk and the kitchen board. Staff and the owner get phone push
when something is theirs to do.

**Scope and contract.** None of this is in the signed Phase 1 Scope of Work, and none of it is in
the Phase 2 change order, which is still unsigned. The SOW change-control clause
(`docs/scope/touch-padel-phase1-scope-of-work.txt:956-963`) requires a written line with its effect
on date and fee before work starts. This work touches four exclusions:
- **Roles beyond the five.** ui-spec:527 says "additional roles are a change request". The six
  roles in `f6a0802` are already on `main`, so they need recording now.
- **Purchase orders and supplier ordering.** SOW:548-556 says "goods received only", and ui-spec:687
  says the same. Shopping list and driver purchases stop short of ordering: they record what was
  bought and hand it to Goods in.
- **Staff shift scheduling or time clock.** SOW:265. Checklists stay task lists, and no report
  ranks people (ui-spec:687, SOW:480).
- **Tournaments.** This is change-order item 4, Milestone 6. The tournament protocol is its
  planning front half only.

It also narrows delivered behaviour. SOW:190 gives the manager the menu, SOW:362-365 delivers sizes
and modifiers "with price differences", ui-spec:377 lists price among the menu editor's editable
fields, and the Touch Shop (change order Milestone 4, awaiting acceptance) lets the manager price
products in Stock ▸ Products. Decisions #41 and #51 take every price of something on sale away from
the manager: menu item sizes, shop products and add-ons change only through the price or promotion
change protocol. Decision #57 adds promotions, court rates (SOW:190 gives the manager "rates") and
the featured-item discount. Decisions #52 and #53 take away putting something new on sale: a new
menu item comes only through product release, launched by the owner, and a manager's new shop
product or paid add-on is saved hidden until the owner approves its price. These locks are new
scope, not part of the protocol engine, so the line names them. So does the kitchen board's new
money-free read (#60): it is a security fix that ships inside line 10, not a separate item.

The change order lists nine numbered items. It also names a dropped tenth item, AI analysis, which
was never given a number and "comes back as its own change order". The day-30 AI write-up (#22) is
not that item, but it sits close to it, so the line must name it. It reuses the edge-function
pattern of `analytics-insights` (gate, template fallback, metering), not the owner assistant, which
stays off. Proposed line for `docs/scope/phase2-change-order-2026-09-21.md` §2, numbered 10 as the
next row of that table:

### 4.2 Built-in ("system") steps vs owner-added generic steps

- **A system step** has a `step_key`. It has its own record fields and a behaviour that runs when it
  **passes** (approved or auto):
  - create the draft menu item
  - record the test stock
  - write the price
  - launch
  - block courts
  - open Add staff
  - apply a change: a size price, a shop launch, an add-on price, a new, edited or re-enabled
    promotion, a court rate or the featured-item discount (§5.4)

  Decided (#39): the owner may rename it (EN and AR), flip "Needs my OK" and edit its checklist.
  - Its actor roles are fixed, because the behaviour's guard depends on them.
  - It cannot be deleted.
  - Whether it is optional comes from its definition in §5, not from the editor.
  - Its order is fixed where it matters (§4.5).
  - On the two owner-only steps, launch and add staff, the switch is hidden: the owner is the
    actor, so the step passes automatically (#50).
  - On the two price steps, product release's `analysis` and price/promo's `numbers`, "Needs my
    OK" is always on and the switch is hidden (#58). `save_protocol_template` refuses a change
    with `PROTOCOL_STEP_FIXED`, so a price is never approved without the owner.
- **A generic step** (`step_key` null) is added by the owner, who is free to add, move and delete
  it (#39). It has:
  - a name in EN and AR
  - actor roles picked from the 9 live non-owner roles (prep is retired, 0157)
  - "Needs my OK"
  - an optional flag
  - checklist items

  Its record is a note, optional photos and the ticks. It has no behaviour. Like every step form it
  works in both apps (#38): on the phone, or on the operator in Protocols (manager, owner) or
  `/tasks` (every other actor), photos included (§6.5).

### 4.3 State machine (the 0112 transition-table pattern)

Two pure `immutable` functions, `app.protocol_step_allowed(from, to)` and
`app.protocol_run_allowed(from, to)`, have the same shape as `app.assistant_job_allowed`
(0112:30-41). Every write goes through one RPC that checks them and raises `INVALID_TRANSITION`,
which `op/lib/errors.ts` already maps.

```
step:  waiting ──(becomes current)──▶ open ──(submit)──▶ submitted ──(approve)─────────────────▶ passed
          │                            ▲                     │ ├──(author = decider: auto)──────▶ passed  (decision 'auto')
          │                            │                     │ ├──(send back + reason)──────────▶ open, round+1
          │                            └──(withdraw)─────────┘ │     or an earlier step reopens (steps between → waiting)
          │                                                    └──(stop + reason)───────────────▶ stopped ⇒ run stopped
          └──(skip + reason; optional steps only; decider)──▶ skipped
       passed ──(send back from a LATER step)──▶ open, round+1        -- the only way out of passed

run:   active ──(last blocking step passes, "now")───────▶ done | live (product release)
       active ──(last blocking step passes, "on date")───▶ scheduled ──(date, ready)──▶ done | live
       scheduled ──(date, not ready | the step's actor cancels the date)──▶ active (last step reopens, actor pushed)
       live ──(day-30 review written)──▶ done
       active ──(a step stopped)──▶ stopped
       active ──(starter withdraws before the first decision)──▶ withdrawn
```

- Each submit adds a new `protocol_submissions` row. A sent submission is never edited. The
  submitter withdraws it instead, while it is undecided (0072:12-29).
- A send-back to an earlier step keeps every earlier round in history. The test loop in product
  release is this mechanism.
- No due dates or reminders in v1.

- **Nobody decides their own submission.** The table CHECK and the RPC (`CANNOT_DECIDE_OWN`, reused
  from 0072:237) enforce it. The automatic pass is the one recorded exception.
- **A reason is required** on send-back, stop and skip (`REASON_REQUIRED`, reused from 0072:243).
  Send-back and stop keep it in `protocol_submissions.decision_note`. A skip usually has no
  submission, so it goes in `protocol_run_steps.skip_note`, with `skipped_by` and `skipped_at`.
- **One-way.** No un-approve. A later step can send the work back, and that is recorded as a new
  round.
- **No override** (#42). At product release's price step and price/promo's numbers step the owner
  approves or sends back (or stops, #8), and never edits the number. The figures on record are
  always the submitter's. A different price means a send-back with the reason.
- **Who may act.** An actor is anyone holding one of `actor_roles` at the venue, or the
  `assigned_to` person when it is set; the test step is assigned to the proposer. Manager and owner
  may cover any step except the two owner-only ones, launch (#40) and add staff, and the record
  shows who submitted.
- **Either app.** Every decision above can be made on the phone or on the operator (#38). Both call
  the same `decide_step` and `skip_step`.

### 4.5 What the owner can edit, and the limits

"How it works" is the owner-only sheet on each Protocols card (§7.1). It edits the template:
- **built-in steps** (#39): the name (EN and AR), "Needs my OK" (hidden on launch and add staff,
  and hidden and always on at the two price steps, #58), and the checklist. Their position only
  within the limits below.
- **the owner's own steps** (#39): add, delete, name, actor roles, "Needs my OK", "Optional", the
  checklist, and position.
- **checklist items**, on either kind: add, rename, remove, reorder, up to 12 per step, EN and AR.
- **order**, using `SortButtons` (`op/components/inputs.tsx`), within the limits below.

`app.save_protocol_template` (owner only; signature in contracts §2.7) checks the whole list, then
bumps `version`. It refuses any change to a built-in step's actors, optional flag or presence, and
to "Needs my OK" on the two price steps and the two owner-only steps (`PROTOCOL_STEP_FIXED`, #58). Decided (#39): order stays fixed where it matters. The server enforces
dependencies, not positions, so a built-in step moves only where no `after` key forbids it. That
leaves two pairs of built-in steps that can swap, because neither depends on the other: price and
marketing in product release, and marketing and courts in a tournament. Treating those two as
"where it does not matter" is a **PROPOSAL**. Every other built-in step keeps its place, and hiring
and price/promo built-in steps do not move at all.
- **First and last are fixed.** The first system step (propose, plan, open position) stays first.
  The terminal system step (launch, ready, add staff, apply) stays last. After product release's
  launch there is no step: the run stays `live` until the day-30 review is saved (§5.1).
- **System steps keep their dependencies.** Each declares `after` keys (§5). A move that breaks one
  is disabled in the editor and refused by the server with `PROTOCOL_ORDER_INVALID`. Generic steps
  move freely between the first and the terminal step.
- **Every text is bilingual.** `TEXT_BOTH_LANGUAGES_REQUIRED`.
- **Concurrent edits fail.** `TEMPLATE_CHANGED` when `p_expected_version` is stale.

**Per-run customisation** is the **owner's only** (#44). The owner may change one running protocol
on the operator's run sheet:
- edit its checklist items (`edit_run_items`, EN and AR required);
- add a generic step (`add_run_step`) before its terminal step.

The manager cannot. Nobody can remove or reorder system steps on a run. An edit to a step that
already passed does not reopen it.

### 5.4 Price or promotion change (`price_promo`): steps a **PROPOSAL**, the price locks decided (#41, #51–#53, #57)

**Run data.** One change per run, of one of eight kinds. The exact record shapes are contracts §2.8.
- **`price`**: sizes of one launched menu item or shop product, each with its new price. On a cafe
  item it may also add new sizes with their prices, because a new size is a price (#51). A shop
  product gets no new size this way: a shop size carries its own stock item, SKU and barcode, so a
  new pack size is a new hidden product launched through `shop_launch`, or the owner adds it
  directly (**PROPOSAL**).
- **`shop_launch`**: a hidden shop product that was never on sale, with a price for every size
  (#53). Apply writes the prices and switches it on. Only a manager or the owner starts one: hidden
  products are not offered to marketing (no screen or target list shows them). That is a UI rule,
  not a data wall: since 0156 any staff session can read its venue's hidden items and their draft
  prices through RLS (0156:749-752, 766-771).
- **`addon_price`**: one or more add-ons (modifiers), each with its new price difference. An add-on
  that was never on sale (a manager's hidden paid add-on) is switched on at apply; an add-on that
  was launched keeps its switch as it is (#51, #53).
- **`promotion`**: a new promotion, in the shape of `app.upsert_promotion` (0067), meaning name
  EN/AR, kind, value, items or categories, days, hours and dates, limits, and an optional code.
- **`promotion_edit`** (#57): an existing promotion, switched on or off, with its new fields in the
  same shape. Nothing is written before apply, and the promotion keeps its switch.
- **`promotion_enable`** (#57): a switched-off promotion, switched on as it stands. New dates are
  a `promotion_edit` first.
- **`rate`** (#57): one court rate rule, new or existing, in the shape of `app.upsert_rate_rule`
  (0071:153): name, one court or all, weekdays, time window, a price per duration, priority, valid
  dates, on or off. Switching a rule off is a `rate` change too, because it changes which price a
  slot gets.
- **`featured_discount`** (#57): the featured item and its discount, X % (0 to 99) off the
  featured item while the hero is in Featured mode. The discount follows the featured item, so the
  change names both. It is live only in Featured mode (`hero_mode = 'featured'`, 0095:169, and the
  same test on the guest menu), so an approved discount above 0 also puts the hero in Featured mode
  at apply; a run may therefore name the stored item and percentage when the hero is not in
  Featured mode, to put that discount live.
- **Every kind:** the reason and the expected effect.

A promotion discounts goods only, never the court fee (0067). Two of the new targets are global
today: `promotions` has no `venue_id`, and `cafe_settings` is keyed by setting alone, so a
promotion or featured-discount change applies at every venue whichever venue's run carries it.
Slice 2 owns scoping both (`PHASE-2-PLAN.md:56, 257`: the `platform_settings` split, promotions
"optionally scoped by rule"). Rate rules carry `venue_id` and are checked against the run's venue.

## 8. Database work (by feature; 0157, money reads plus prep retired on the server, is committed in `ed5c2e3` and is not part of this plan)

**Numbering** (binding, #37). No ordinal is reserved, and this plan lists migrations by name and
dependency only.
- **Why.** `check-migrations.mjs` refuses a new migration whose ordinal is not above the highest on
  `origin/main` (`migration-ordinal-not-max`), one that repeats an ordinal already in the directory
  (`migration-duplicate-ordinal`), and one whose version does not sort after every file on
  `origin/main` (`migration-out-of-order`). Milestone 1 slice 2 is built alongside this work in the
  same directory, so any number written into a plan in advance goes stale.
- **While building** (contracts §1.4). A migration is known by its feature name (the table below)
  and lives as a draft in `packages/db/supabase/drafts/<feature>.sql`, outside `migrations/`, so
  two lanes never take the same provisional ordinal and no lane's `db:reset` runs another lane's
  half-built file. Drafts are re-runnable and applied to the shared local stack with `docker exec`.
  Its dollar tags are `$<name>_<feature>$` until commit.
- **At commit** (contracts §1.3, §1.4). The draft moves into `migrations/` with the next free
  ordinal: 1 + the highest of every file in the directory, `HEAD`, and `origin/main` after
  `git fetch origin main`. Its version is the commit day plus that ordinal, later than every existing
  file (`<YYYYMMDD><ordinal, 6 digits>_<name>.sql`). The header comment and every dollar tag take the
  same ordinal in the same step (`$name_0NNN$`). Commit order is therefore ordinal order.
- **Dependencies.** A migration commits after everything in its "Commits after" column, whichever
  lane owns it (contracts §2.2).
- **Re-issues across the two streams.** Where this plan and slice 2 re-issue the same function,
  whichever commits second copies the other's body (the latest-body rule below). Check at commit
  time, not when the lane started. The known overlap is `analytics_courts_summary` and
  `analytics_courts_cafe` (latest 0147) and `report_courts` (latest 0097), which
  `event_court_blocks` re-issues and slice 2 restarts against (`PHASE-2-PLAN.md:12`). Round 10
  adds three likely ones: `upsert_rate_rule` and `set_cafe_setting` (`price_promo`), which slice
  2's venue axis and `platform_settings` split may re-issue, and the four order-side read policies
  (`kitchen_money_reads`), whose venue axis slice 2 owns for leaf tables (0136 header).
- **Today (2026-09-25).** 0168 is the highest ordinal on disk. Waves 0–2 are committed as
  0155–0168. `origin/main` carries up to 0161, but hosted has applied only 0155–0160: 0161 waits
  for operator 0.2.20 on every kitchen machine. 0162–0168 are local.
- **Committed means re-issue, never edit.** The engine (0162–0164) and the staff-page data
  (0165–0168) are committed migrations. A role-spec change to any of them is a new migration that
  copies the committed body and changes only what it must. These are F's `tournament_desk_start`,
