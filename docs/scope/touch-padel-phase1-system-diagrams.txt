1 · The System at a Glance
AUDIENCE: EVERYONE
WHAT THIS SHOWS
Three apps, one shared database. Guests use the mobile app for courts and the website for the cafe. Staff use one Windows desktop app whose screens depend on the
person's role. Everything reads and writes the same records, and changes push out live — no refreshing.

2 · Who Sees What — The Five Roles
AUDIENCE: EVERYONE
WHAT THIS SHOWS
Each staff role sees only what its permissions allow, and the rules live in the database itself — they can't be bypassed by opening a different screen. Discounts, voids,
overrides and stock adjustments all land in a permanent audit log with the person, the change, and the reason.

3 · How a Guest Books a Court
AUDIENCE: EVERYONE
WHAT THIS SHOWS
A slot is briefly held while the guest completes the booking, so nobody else can grab it mid-checkout. The moment it's confirmed, it appears on the desk calendar. Payment
stays at the desk — there is no online payment in Phase 1.

4 · Why Double-Booking Is Impossible
AUDIENCE: EVERYONE · KEY GUARANTEE
WHAT THIS SHOWS
All court time — app bookings, desk bookings, holds, maintenance — goes into one table the database itself refuses to overlap. A clash isn't a bug we hope to catch; it's a write
that physically cannot be stored. Proven by automated tests firing simultaneous requests at the same slot.

5 · How a Guest Orders at the Cafe
AUDIENCE: EVERYONE
WHAT THIS SHOWS
No app, no account: the phone camera scans the table's QR code and the menu opens already bound to that table, in the guest's language. The order flows to the kitchen
screen live, and status updates on the guest's page. Ordering is not paying — everything settles at the desk.

6 · Life of a Cafe Order
AUDIENCE: STAFF + TEAM
WHAT THIS SHOWS
Every order moves through defined stages. Nothing disappears: a void after the kitchen has the ticket is recorded as waste, and refunds (manager only) reverse the stock
movement. This is what makes the day-close and variance numbers trustworthy.

8 · Closing the Day
AUDIENCE: STAFF + MANAGER
WHAT THIS SHOWS
The day cannot close while a tab is still open on the floor. Cash is counted against what the system expects, the card terminal batch is entered for reconciliation, and every
discount, void, refund and waste entry is listed with the person who authorised it.

9 · When the Internet Drops — Degraded Mode
AUDIENCE: EVERYONE · KEY GUARANTEE
WHAT THIS SHOWS
The till and kitchen keep working from a local queue; nothing typed is lost even in a power cut. The app and website are blocked from near-term bookings so a slot can never
be sold twice while offline. On reconnect, the queue replays exactly once and totals reconcile.

10 · Technical Architecture
AUDIENCE: TEAM
WHAT THIS SHOWS
TypeScript end to end. Supabase Postgres is the single source of truth with row-level security, the booking exclusion constraint, and append-only ledgers. Realtime channels
push tickets, statuses and freed slots to every client. Types are generated from the schema, so a schema change breaks the build — not the venue.

11 · Data Model (ER Diagram)
AUDIENCE: TEAM
WHAT THIS SHOWS
The core entities and how they relate. Notable: one RESERVATION table with an exclusion constraint carries all court time; each booking stores the RATE_RULE that priced it;
stock is an append-only ledger of movements against expiry-dated batches (first-expiring-first); a cafe TAB can be charged to a court booking.

12 · Delivery Plan — Six Weeks
AUDIENCE: EVERYONE
WHAT THIS SHOWS
Four parallel build tracks over four weeks, then two weeks reserved for store review, defect fixing, training and handover. Track A (platform) runs a few days ahead in week 1
— it is the foundation the other tracks build on. A working build is demonstrated at the end of every week.
